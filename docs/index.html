Chip strategy
LimitlessUse
Strong consensus this is the best race of the season for Limitless — same as 2025. Midfield drivers barely score (avg 5.26 pts at Monaco over last 3 years), so the gap between normal teams and Limitless teams is maximized. FormulaFantasyHub says even with 2 toxic assets dropping 0.6M, the chip is still worth using. Few viable Limitless weekends remain (maybe Silverstone or Hungary). If you have multiple assets expected to lose value, this is the time. Caveat: if heavy rain is forecast, consider holding — but current forecasts suggest mostly dry qualifying.
Final FixWait
Excellent option if your 2x driver qualifies P4 or lower. FanAmp specifically recommends keeping ~0.6M extra budget to swap to the pole sitter (e.g., swap Leclerc for Antonelli or vice versa). Worked brilliantly at Baku 2025 when Piastri qualified P7. At Monaco where qualifying = race result, this chip has outsized value.
WildcardSave
Not the ideal week to wildcard unless your team is in dire shape. Multiple creators (ZachGP, ReinFantasy) suggest saving for the dense European swing. ReinFantasy used his wildcard before Canada specifically to set up for Monaco with the right assets.
No NegativeSave
Low-scoring weekend means less downside to protect against. DNF risk exists but is average (~2.5 per race). Better saved for a sprint weekend or a race with higher variance.
Watch before deadline
1
FP1/FP2 performance of Mercedes around Monaco — are they competitive in slow-speed corners or do they fall back significantly? This determines Antonelli vs Leclerc as 2x captain.
2
Weather forecast evolution — Friday looks wetter than Saturday/Sunday. If qualifying is wet, it could scramble the grid and make Final Fix essential. Rain on Sunday would increase safety car probability and pit lane chaos with 22 cars.
3
Aston Martin and Cadillac reliability in practice — determines whether Stroll and Bottas are safe budget holds or DNF risks.
4
FIA compression ratio testing effective June 1st — may not show impact at Monaco (low power sensitivity) but could change competitive order from Barcelona onwards.
5
Straight mode zone placements — unusual circuit may create unexpected deployment patterns that favor certain cars.
6
McLaren strategy decisions — after Canada's disastrous intermediate tire call, will they play it safe or try something aggressive at a track where pit strategy is the only way to gain positions?
Race arc
Monaco is the lowest-scoring race of the season for midfield drivers and the most polarizing weekend for chip strategy. It serves as the gateway to the dense European swing: Barcelona follows immediately and is a high-downforce, high-overtaking circuit where Mercedes should reassert dominance — making any kneejerk sales of Mercedes assets at Monaco premature. The June 1st FIA compression ratio testing may not show effects until Barcelona/Austria, but could reshape the competitive order for the remainder of the European leg. Budget management is critical: assets like Colapinto are approaching 10M and may need to be sold after Austria. Players who successfully deploy Limitless here and protect their budget builders will have a significant advantage heading into the Silverstone sprint weekend. Lawson becomes an excellent buy candidate AFTER Monaco when his rolling average resets.
