Watch before deadline
1
Charles Leclerc health status — reported vision issues post-Monza crash; Ferrari says he'll race but will reassess after FP1. If he sits out, Antonio Giovinazzi replaces him and Ferrari becomes an avoid.
2
Leclerc engine penalty — Ferrari claim PU is saved, but trust is low. Monitor official announcements through Friday/Saturday. A grid penalty would actually boost his F1 Fantasy upside via overtakes.
3
FP1/FP2 running order — completely new circuit means Friday practice is critical for establishing the real pecking order. Adjust team at Saturday deadline.
4
Track evolution and grip levels — green track will rubber in significantly across F2/F3 support races and F1 sessions. Big lap time evolution expected between FP1 and FP3.
5
Aston Martin reliability — double DNF at Monza from mechanical issues. James Vowles warned Madrid is physically demanding for cars. Watch FP1-FP3 for any signs of stress on Honda PU.
6
Ferrari filming day advantage — Ferrari ran an F1 car at Madring in July, giving them setup correlation data no other team has. Could translate to faster setup convergence and stronger qualifying.
7
Energy deployment mapping — new track means teams are relying on simulator data for energy strategies. McLaren has struggled with energy mapping recently. Watch which teams get this right in practice.
8
Pit lane chaos potential — 550m pit lane with a 100m building in the middle and a braking zone inside it. Potential for unusual pit lane incidents.
9
Curb issues flagged in F3 testing — could cause damage to F1 cars, particularly affecting ride height and floor. Watch for broken suspension or floor damage in practice.
Race arc
The Spanish GP at the brand-new Madring is the highest-variance race of the second half of the season. With most top managers having already burned their premium chips (3x, Limitless), the game enters a grind phase where weekly optimization and avoiding DNFs matter more than chip plays. This makes the No Negative chip especially valuable here. The results at Madrid will reveal which cars handle high-load, cornering-intensive street-style circuits — directly informing strategy for Baku (Round 17), another street circuit with walls and high DNF rates. Williams' season-defining update arrives in Baku, potentially reshuffling the budget tier. Albon and Lindblad are positioned as holds through both races. Aston Martin's reliability trajectory at Madrid will determine whether Stroll/Alonso are viable going forward or need permanent exits. Pierre Gasly and Alpine's upswing could crystallize into a must-own asset by Baku/Malaysia if the Mercedes PU advantage continues at power-sensitive circuits.
